Start with the biggest myth

Higher tension ≠ more power.

The string bed is a trampoline. Looser strings flex more on impact and throw the shuttle back for you. Tighter strings barely move, so the power must come from your own swing speed. That's why tighter strings give control, not free power.

The racket itself · weight

What "3U / 4U / 5U" means

The U-grade is the racket's weight class - lower number = heavier. (Approximate unstrung ranges; they vary slightly by brand.)

2U90–94 g
Heaviest, most powerful & stable. Most mass behind smashes and clears, but tiring and slower to swing. For strong, attacking players.
3U85–89 g
The classic all-round weight. Good power with manageable handling. The most common singles choice and a safe pick.
4U80–84 g
Light and fast. Quicker swings and reactions with still-decent power. Hugely popular, especially for doubles and modern fast play.
5U75–79 g
Very light & maneuverable. Easiest on the arm, fastest hands, but less mass for power. Great for beginners, juniors and quick net/defence play.

Rule of thumb: heavier = more power but slower and more tiring; lighter = faster and easier but you generate power from speed and technique, not mass. Beginners and anyone with arm/shoulder strain usually do best at 4U–5U.

      By player level

      What to prioritise as you grow

      Beginner

      Forgiveness first

      You want the biggest, most forgiving sweet spot and effortless power while your technique is still forming. Low tension does both. A durable string means you're not restringing constantly, and a lighter, easy-to-swing racket protects your arm and helps you learn clean contact.

      Start refining

      Your contact is more consistent, so you can trade a little forgiveness for feel. Nudge tension up, move to a balanced or power string, and choose a balance that matches your game - head-heavy if you attack, head-light if you defend.

      Advanced

      Control & precision

      You hit the centre cleanly and consistently, so a small sweet spot is fine and even desirable. Higher tension gives sharp placement and feel; a thin string gives crisp response and the steepest, fastest shots. Set weight and balance to your role.

      Maintenance

      When to restring

      Rule of thumb: restring about as many times per year as you play per week. Play 3× a week → restring ~3× a year, minimum - even if it never snaps.

      Signs your string is dead

      • The crisp "ping" is gone and hits sound dull or muddy.
      • You're hitting shorter for the same effort - power has quietly leaked away.
      • The shuttle feels like it slides or sits on the strings instead of springing off.
      • Visible fraying, notching or flat spots where the strings cross.
      • Strings have shifted out of place and don't snap back.

      Don't wait for a break - a dead string steals power and control well before it snaps. And get it strung on a proper machine; even, quality stringing holds tension far better than a cheap rush job.

      The whole guide in 7 lines

      • 01Lower tension = more power (bigger trampoline). Higher = more control, needs a fast swing.
      • 02Thinner string = more pop, breaks faster. Thicker = durable, less pop.
      • 03Beginners: low tension + thick durable string + big sweet spot. Advanced: tighter + thinner.
      • 04Lighter / head-light racket = fast hands & defence. Heavier / head-heavy = smash power.
      • 05Backcourt depth & power → lower tension + repulsion string (and good technique).
      • 06Stay 1–2 lb under the frame's max; keep budget / very light frames lower.
      • 07Restring ~(times you play per week) per year - dead strings feel weak.
